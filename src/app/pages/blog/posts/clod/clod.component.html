The Claudening

August 23, 2026

Frog's a-boilin'.

I have noticed an increasing amount of people whose Anthropic employee friends gift them 2-6 months of the Max plan to get them hooked.
Once in, they buy their own ChatGPT subscription, subscribe to Theo T3, write total slop using Claude Code and not even readin the output, and even get stressed by token limits, trying their best to consume their window before they reset but also not end up without tokens by the end of the week.

Now, I've always been opposed to these machines, and I thought the sentiment mainly stayed the same if not got worse as time went on, but it turns out I grossly overestimated the average person's object persistence.

As soon as you hang a free subscription in front off someone, they'll bite, and start saying stuff like: It's really damn useful, but there is a line between useful and replacing our own skills.
Maybe if you don't know how to write or how to code and really, really don't care about learning.
But for everyone else, I beg you, keep those neurons firing and don't turn into a slop machine pulling dopamine addict.
From that one time Fedi went nuts over slop-generated sprite companions for Codex to this current mess I've seen people I saw as friends get into...

I'll always be part of the human resistance.

Now more than ever we need humans to keep their skills, to sharpen them, to prove to these companies selling us tokens to spin a stochastic parrot's random number generator that mankind has something they'll never have: feelings, intelligence, creativity, the ability to make art, and to connect with people.
If you need to churn out a webpage edit or use these machines to make a living, this is not about you. There is such a thing as over-reliance on technology, and as mindful usage.
If your employer forces you to tokenmaxx and evaluates your productivity on how fast you burn through tokens, I understand, but if you have a choice, please don't do this to yourself.
I know there's always forces above us, stronger than us.
This is about mental replacement, substituting with chance what we can do with our minds.
Please, don't let your brain rust.

I watched a really good human-written, human-produced video on the biases at play when we hear the overwhelmingly confident tone of an LLM, and that we should always be skeptical of the output of these machines, and that we should always fact-check them.

You can watch it here.

None of this means these machines can never produce good output, but they're stochastic parrots.
In the hands of someone careful, someone clever and that checks what the Thousand Monkeys with a Typewriter do, they can be a decent tool.
The issue is, reliance, not checking the output, winging it and praying, not understanding, losing context, practice, rusting.
Blame the Human, not the tool.
And yeah blame the multi trillion dollar corporations for trying to make revenue via dependence and addiction, possibly hushering in multiple financial crises.

Another aspect that's been fucking with me is not just the mental replacement, job displacement and addiction, but the increasing tolerance.
If a meme is funny enough, most people do not care that it has machine-generated visuals, or that the song has no high-end and generic vocals made by Suno, no matter how bad they are to onlookers who do not get the joke.
If a website works, people overlook its design and accessibility flaws, and if a song is catchy enough, people overlook the fact that it was made by a machine — like for ages they've done Taylor Swift's crap — and gobble it up. Critical thinking and caring for what happens behind the scenes is dead.

From my friend that made an ugly kinklist whose UI was clearly not human-tailored to Fenix Flexin and Tyga generating whole 80s albums (and that's stretching the definition quite a lot) with this stuff.
So much of Spotify's top 50 is now AI-generated, and people don't care, especially the average Country and Christian music listener.
I despise all of this. It really gives weight to the word slop. Music has been turned into a commodity, churned out and consumed.
And I hate how many websites and apps are getting redesigns and new features via LLMs, how many people tolerate it, and how many people use them.

The last thing I noticed is how these machines are disproportionately used by people to cut on costs, to avoid hiring a human, especially for graphic design.
Going throughout Italy, some of Germany and the Netherlands I noticed just how much more AI-generated content is present in Italy than the others.
From Fiat Panda ads to pizzerias' pictures on Google Maps, from store logos to social media posts. In a country of people predominantly over 40 and with a shrinking middle class, tolerance for laziness and low quality output is high.

I do not want to live in a world where content is divided by high and low quality, where the 1% can afford to hire humans and the 99% are forced to use machines out of economic necessity.
