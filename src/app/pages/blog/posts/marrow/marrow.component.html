marroW — brakence

October 26, 2026

Four years later, brakence digs into himself.

Introduction

After a four year wait, posters in NY and LA, two websites, a bunch of promo TikToks and a TikTok Live, two ARGs, him roleplaying with himself in his Discord server, and 4 singles, Randall Findell releases marroW, his most heartfelt project yet.

Digging deep into himself, it covers themes that range from accepting death and environmental guilt to cutting abusive behaviour out of himself.


ready or noT

On September 25th 2026, brakence, after teasing us on Twitter, released ready or noT, the first single of this release cycle. I instantly resonated with the lyrics, and the visual and sonic aesthetics of this track.
Homie is going through something, a partner of mine said, after experiencing the music video. This song introduces the physical forms, marionettes, of butcheR and jesteR, two of the characters at play in this album. This track alone is enough to let us understand that these two creatures torment Randy's psyche.

Music video for ready or noT

The music video features jesteR's disembodied glove slipping a substance into Randy's stew, and butcheR trying to kill jesteR. As this goes on, we see Randy's body get pulled by strings, him grabbing an axe: a pretty on-the-nose metaphor for this internal conflict.

While I will not cover all the material I previously mentioned, and highly suggest you experience it yourself, I will continue giving my interpretation of this record as we go on, so this is your last warning when it comes to spoilers.


crystallized & death rolL

I want you to picture this: someone you dearly love woke you up to listen to the new brakence singles, and the first track is crystallized, probably Randy's only love song without bitter notes. It seamlessly flows between comparing his love interest to a muse, and on-the-nose innuendos about being inside this woman.
His voice is smooth like silk and sweet like honey, doing his trademark falsetto, this time over guitars, synths, and a very percussive beat with an addictive bassline. A really good technique used is tying the absence of those harsh percussive elements to the volume of the melodic elements, like an inverse sidechain.

I really like death rolL, picking up the beautiful imagery of the (original version of) knot-here.world and its desert, prying something out of a crocodile's mouth... The production on this one is addictive, poppy, groovy, moody, aggressive.
It portrays fear of death, feeling dead, brakence killing part of himself, feeling unworthy of his place as an artist and a person, taking you through the motions with him: guilt, fear, repeating, until contemplating the beauty of life, self-judgement turning into self-assurance, culminating in uncertainty about his stability...
I've been told death rolL leaked previously, and I like the current version more.


Problem Child

This is one of the tracks I liked least, granted, it did eventually grow on me. While I do enjoy its lyrics, although not as much as the rest of the album, I find the production a bit generic. Randy's vocal performance is lackluster and it doesn't do the writing justice.

I like the metaphor of what humanity has done to nature applied to brakence's psyche, and him feeling forsaken, like all he can do is betray nature herself, begging her to save him from himself, for forgiveness.


bitE & body

After an update to knot-here.world, we were presented with a multiple-choice game. Ready for guessworK? All paths in this game led to certain death, no matter which choices you made. Up to a coin toss, yet you get to explore both sides of the coin, and play both characters.

bitE is more of what death rolL was, while body starts off calmer, before warping into a plea for help. Both tracks expose a side of Randy that he desperately wants to get rid of. The first is more of a direct depiction of a fight between these two shards of being, while the second sounds like self-castigation.


The rest of the album

If you're a first time brakence listener, while I do not think listening to punk2 is necessary, I do believe you should get acquainted with hypochondriac to enjoy marroW to its fullest, and that marroW requires a few listens to be processed completely.


A lot of the production on this album can only be described as reminiscent of analog horror, wavering between Lo-Fi and scratchy samples that fight for attention between one another, without taking away from Randy's emotionally charged vocal performance. If anything, at times, his voice is the only comforting thing in this project, which then betrays the unprepared listener, yelling, chanting painful words.


jesteR was the first track to be teased, with a short sample of the intro and Randy yelling I'm still here on still-here.world. This track is about the guilt brakence has for the music he's made and is still making, about a part of himself he hates, about not wanting to hurt others, and it switches so effortlessly between energetic and draining exposition.
It transitions into tougH, a track both about wanting to be thicker skinned and feeling unworthy. All these records are starting to make me worry brakence might have some kind of personality disorder... Boss Fight reinforces this, with him hating another part of himself, the one that wallows in suffering, the edgy part of him.


For those of you that have followed the rollout, I feel like this album's multiple characters manifest as also multiple voices, multiple personalities, different production choices and vibes. I personally have not found that to be consistent with the song titles, but maybe someone can draw the line for me.


While hypochondriac was a psychedelic experience with smooth transitions, marroW lacks a decent bit in the transition department. It feels like some tracks were shoehorned in, specifically Problem Child. It gives me a bit of whiplash after death rolL, and doesn't flow smoothly into wishing well.


I really like wishing well. If you're an underscores fan, this is basically brakence's Tell Me (U Want It): begging a love interest to open up about their feelings, to stop being so skittish and shy, to be upfront and give in, to stop tiptoeing around the conversation and just SAY. IT. ALREADY!!!


sinkflY is a grim depiction of doubting if you're a good person, of feeling like all your behaviours do is steal people away and ruin their lives. brakence thinks he will take away everything his partner loves, but also, he's made himself this partner's everything, hence, coming clear, What if everything you love isn't what you thought it was?
What if this sinkflY is attached to Randy's body, draining him, and is not just imagery in the cover art but a real being? He opens the song as the bug, showing us he is what he wants to destroy. The guilt in the opening verse, him feeling that he must admit to his actions. Everything about this song, really, even the voice cracks. It's beautiful, toxic, healing, disgusting.


Moving on, the juxtaposition of crystallized and traitoR: calling someone my lady and then opening up about having a deep-rooted fear of commitment. It's wonderful, really.
The ending lines of crystallized, we all decompose, segue into traitoR with how Randy is getting older and feels like he should've taken a vow already, but is unable to tell this girl he loves her, guilt making him call himself a traitor, feeling trapped by this situationship that just wants something serious. Great storytelling and self-awareness.


heart is probably brakence's most mellow track to date. Helene's Solo is a haunting instrumental noise track and I am so in love with it.
The intro of with or without it — the last track of the album — starts with a voice saying I am one, finally giving resolution to this arc of him diving into multiple entities.


You too should get to some lovin before it's too late, dear reader, as this record was a harsh journey, not in quality but in themes.


Verdict

Rating: 9 out of 10.
